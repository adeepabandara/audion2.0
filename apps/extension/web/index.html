This is a simulated call so you can try Ola without joining a meeting. The real thing is the Chrome extension, which puts Ola into your Google Meet, Teams or Zoom call.

How to try it

  1. Open Ola from the small pill at the bottom right of the call (or the Ola icon in the browser bar), switch it on, and allow the camera. Video stays in your browser's memory and is never stored.
  2. Make one American Sign Language sign, then lower your hands. Ola sends a short clip for recognition.
  3. Read the caption and listen to the voice. Turn your sound on.

Recognition can take a few seconds per sign. If you do not sign, you can still look at how it works below.

Four sentences to try

Make the signs in order, lowering your hands after each one. After the last sign, wait a moment and Ola builds and speaks the sentence. The wording may vary a little.

Meeting context

The call above has live captions from a short made-up meeting. Every so often Gemini summarises them, and that summary helps Gemma and Gemini word your sentences for what the meeting is about. Try signing WHERE then TIME and compare the sentence with the context switched off. In a real Meet call the same thing reads Meet's own captions (turn them on with the CC button). Live pipeline

See which model did each step, and how long it took. Hands are found on your device by MediaPipe. Then the sign is named on your device by the sign model, with no cloud call. Gemma (or Gemini as the backup) writes the sentence, and Google Text-to-Speech speaks it.

    No camera? Test with a video

    Pick a video of someone signing. It plays in place of the camera, and nothing is uploaded: it stays in your browser. Then turn Ola on. Ola recognises one sign at a time, so the video needs a short rest between signs, and only the 22 signs below can be named.



    Hear the voices

    Ola speaks with Google Cloud Text-to-Speech. Choose a voice in Ola's settings (the gear in the panel). Press a button to hear it.

    Sentences written on this device

    Gemma 4 is Google's open model. When you turn Ola on, your browser starts getting it (about 2 GB, once, then kept in your browser) and, if it supports WebGPU, Gemma writes your sentences right here with no server call for that step. Until it is ready, and if it cannot run, Gemini writes them. Untick the box to stop it.



    How it works

    • Hand tracking runs on your device (MediaPipe).
    • A small sign model, trained on isolated ASL signs, names each sign on your device in milliseconds. No video is sent anywhere for this step.
    • Gemma, Google's open model, writes the sentence from several signs, on your device. Gemini on Google Cloud writes it until Gemma has loaded, or if your browser cannot run it. Google Cloud Text-to-Speech speaks it.
    • Ola also works as a Chrome extension that speaks into Google Meet calls.
