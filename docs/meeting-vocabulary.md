# Ola for work meetings: draft vocabulary

**Status: reviewed and approved by the project owner on 2026-10-18.** The reviewer's name and whether they are an
ASL-fluent or Deaf professional were not recorded here; if the review was not by one, get that confirmation before
the list is used to make claims about accuracy. Written by the assistant. The words are English labels for ideas;
which sign (or fingerspelled word, or phrase) people actually use at work can differ by region and person.

## Why this list exists

Ola recognises 22 signs today. They come from PopSign ASL v1.0, whose 250 signs all come from the MacArthur-Bates CDI,
the first concepts taught to a child. So the data is rich in animals, food and family and thin on work. Facts used here:
the 250-sign list is on the dataset page (https://signdata.cc.gatech.edu/view/datasets/popsign_v1_0/); the licence is
CC BY 4.0 (credit Georgia Tech and the Deaf Professional Arts Network).

## A. Already in Ola (22)

hello, bye, yes, no, please, thank you, wait, why, who, where, now, later, tomorrow, time, finish, think, look, listen,
talk, fine, same, not.

## D. Phrases the speech should get exactly right

Ola can map a short run of signs to one polished sentence (as it does now for "please wait", "thank you, goodbye"). Candidates for a first set:

1. I have a question.
2. Can you repeat that, please?
3. I agree.
4. I don't understand.
5. Please go ahead.
6. One moment, please.
7. Thank you, everyone.
8. Sorry, I'm late.
9. Can we do this tomorrow?
10. What time is the meeting?
11. I'll send it later.
12. Let's finish this now.

## E. Questions for the reviewers

1. Which of the ideas in B and C do Deaf professionals actually need in meetings? Which are missing? Which are not signs people would use?
2. For each chosen idea, which sign or fingerspelled form is common at work, and are there regional or personal variants Ola must accept?
3. For each phrase in D, what is a natural ASL form, and what English sentence should Ola speak? (ASL does not follow English word order, and questions use facial grammar that Ola cannot see.)
4. What should Ola do when it is unsure in a meeting: stay silent, show a preview, offer the second guess?
5. Which words must never be spoken by mistake (names, numbers, dates, commitments)?
